Read in a school calendar

Paste it, or open the file. Nothing is guessed about what a date means — you say, once, and it goes in.

…or open a file:

What you teach, in your own words: Given to the reader so it can set aside what is plainly somebody else's. Left empty, nothing is set aside and you see everything.

Year, for any line that doesn't say one: Taken from the document. A table that puts the year on its own line leaves the dates without one, and being twelve months out is the sort of mistake nothing else would show you.

Month the grid is showing: Worked out from which weekday its first column is and how long the month runs. Change it if it's wrong.

It reads the words and the dates; what each one means to your week is still yours to say.

Mark the day the students come back as lessons start: a timetable typed in with no dates on it runs for ever — through the set-up week, the holidays and next July — so that is the line that says when the teaching actually happens. Where a holiday arrives as two lines, a start and an end, tick runs on to on the first and it becomes every day between.

Book time off

Days you won't be doing anything — away, resting, busy with something else. Nothing gets planned into them, and everything else moves to fit around them. Telling it early is the whole point.
